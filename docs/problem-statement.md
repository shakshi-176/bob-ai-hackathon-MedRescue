# \# Problem Statement

# 

# \## Background

# Healthcare supply chains - particularly pharmacies and hospitals - regularly manage large volumes of medicine with fixed expiry dates. Inventory is typically tracked per-location, with no visibility into stock levels or expiry timelines at other nearby facilities. This isolated tracking creates blind spots: a facility may be sitting on soon-to-expire medicine while another facility nearby is actively short of that same medicine.

# 

# \## The Problem

# Pharmacies and hospitals discard significant quantities of unexpired medicine every year simply because no system flags it in time and connects it to a facility that needs it. For example, a pharmacy may have 150 units of insulin expiring in 10 days, while a hospital 4km away has zero units in stock and a steady weekly demand for it - yet neither party is aware of the other's situation until it's too late to act.

# 

# \## Who is Affected

# \- \*\*Pharmacy and hospital inventory managers\*\* who must manually track expiry dates across hundreds of medicine batches, often discovering expiring stock only during periodic manual audits

# \- \*\*Patients\*\* at nearby facilities who are told a medicine is unavailable, even though usable stock exists a short distance away

# \- \*\*District health networks and hospital chains\*\* that absorb the combined financial cost of wasted stock and emergency reordering for the same medicine, simultaneously

# 

# \## Why It Matters

# Discarded medicine is a direct financial loss and a regulatory/environmental disposal cost for the facility that held it. At the same time, the facility facing a shortage may delay patient treatment or pay a premium for emergency procurement. Because both problems stem from the same root cause - lack of cross-facility visibility - solving one largely solves the other, making this a high-leverage point to intervene.

# 

# \## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

# Standard inventory management systems track stock levels within a single facility and alert based on fixed quantity thresholds (e.g., "reorder when stock falls below 10 units") - they have no awareness of expiry timelines or of other facilities' stock and needs. Manual coordination between facilities (phone calls, WhatsApp groups) is slow, inconsistent, and relies on staff remembering to check and reach out before it's too late. No existing system proactively predicts expiry risk and automatically matches it to real demand elsewhere.
